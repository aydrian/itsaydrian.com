Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan really felt like stepping inside my Animal Crossing game. If you have played it, you know the museum: you catch fish and bugs, donate them, and slowly fill up the aquarium wing. Kaiyukan is that, but real, with one giant tank in the middle of the building and a whale shark swimming around in it.

So I filled the log: fourteen catches, from a ray in the fish tunnel to the whale shark (and one diver). It had been a rainy week in Osaka, so an indoor day made sense. Here is how to get there, the route through the building from the top floor down, and what I would tell a friend before they book.

## Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan at a glance

- **Take the Osaka Metro Chuo Line to Osakako Station.** Exit 1 is about a five-minute walk from the aquarium. Head for the giant Ferris wheel and you will find it.
- **Book a timed entry online.** Ours was 1 o'clock. Same-day tickets at the counter may not get you in at the time you want.
- **Entry was ¥2,800, about $17.** That was on a weekday in November. Kaiyukan prices change by date, so check yours when you book.
- **Start at the top.** An escalator takes you to the top floor, and then you walk down around one huge tank, the Pacific Ocean.
- **Give it at least an hour.** Getting from the route map at the entrance to the gift shop took us about an hour, at a steady pace with one ice cream stop. Plan for longer if you like to linger at the glass.

## How to get to Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan

We took the Chuo Line out to the port. Kaiyukan's <a href="https://www.kaiyukan.com/language/eng/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official site</a> puts it about five minutes on foot from Exit 1 of Osakako Station. Walk toward Tempozan Marketplace and use the Ferris wheel as your landmark.

Kaiyukan sits right on the harbor, next to that giant Ferris wheel. The building is covered in fish, with a whale shark painted up the front. You can't miss it.

I was warned ahead of time that there were a lot of group tours that day, so it could be a little crowded. Sure enough, the plaza out front was full of school groups lining up for class photos.

## The trick: start at the top and spiral down

Before you go in, there is a map of the whole route on the wall, and it is worth a minute. The route only goes one way: you start at the top and walk your way down, around the Pacific Ocean.

The middle of the building is one giant tank, and the walkway spirals down around it floor by floor. In Animal Crossing terms, this is the museum. So I'm filling the log.

## The Kaiyukan route, stop by stop

### 1. Aqua Gate: the fish tunnel

You walk in through a fish tunnel called the Aqua Gate, with water on both sides and over your head. First catch: a ray, right over my head.

### 2. The escalator to the top floor

After the tunnel, an escalator takes you all the way up. You start in a small forest room, the Japan Forest on the map, before the water rooms begin.

### 3. Antarctica, the Tasman Sea, and the Great Barrier Reef

Every room up here is a different part of the Pacific. Antarctica has the big penguins. The Tasman Sea has dolphins. And the Great Barrier Reef is just showing off.

## Is Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan worth it?

For me, yes. It cost around $17, and the big glass tank in the middle is the reason to go. Walking up and down around it, with all the creatures swimming past, is the closest I have come to walking into my Animal Crossing museum. If you are in Osaka, I recommend checking it out.

## Tips for visiting Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan

- **Book your timed entry online.** You pick a date and time when you buy the e-ticket. Ours was 1 o'clock. The <a href="https://www.kaiyukan.com/language/eng/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official site</a> says same-day tickets at the counter may not get you in at the time you want, and you might have to wait for a slot.
- **Check the price for your date.** Kaiyukan uses dynamic pricing, so the price moves with demand. Ours was ¥2,800 on a weekday.
- **Expect school groups on a weekday.** We went on a Tuesday, and the plaza was full of them.
- **Go all the way to the top first.** Then take your time on the way down.
- **Save room for the ice cream.** Get the Jinbei, the blue-and-white whale shark cone. The blue half is Ramune.
- **It works on a rainy day.** Except for the short walk from the station, the whole visit is indoors.
