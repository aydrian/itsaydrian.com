Fukusaki is a small town in Hyogo, about half an hour past Himeji on a local train. It is also the hometown of Kunio Yanagita, the father of Japanese folklore studies, and the town leans into it hard. There are yokai sitting on benches all over town: a tengu on a laptop, an oni taking a selfie, a red bean washer playing a Nintendo Switch. Each bench has a QR code that tells you that yokai's story, pulled from Yanagita's books.

I rented a ¥500 e-bike, grabbed the town's hand-drawn map, and spent about three hours hunting down all 18 yokai on it. I got a little lost along the way. This is the guide I wish I had that morning.

## The short version

- **Get to Himeji, then take the JR Bantan Line to Fukusaki Station.** Roughly an hour from Osaka to Himeji on the JR Special Rapid, then about 30 minutes on the Bantan Line.
- **Rent the e-bike.** It was ¥500, it is right by the station, and the benches are spread out much farther than the map makes it look.
- **Don't navigate by the paper map.** Search Google Maps for "Yokai Bench:" plus the yokai's name and ask for bike directions. Every pin is in the table below.
- **Give it about three hours.** That is how long all 18 took me, including one hill and some backtracking.

## How to get to Fukusaki

From Osaka, take the JR Special Rapid west to Himeji. At Himeji, switch to the JR Bantan Line heading north and ride it to Fukusaki Station. Plan on about an hour and a half, plus the transfer.

Say hi to the kappa when you arrive. His name is Gajiro, he lives in a water tank in front of the station, and every 15 minutes he rises out of the water holding a welcome sign. The shows run at 7, 22, 37, and 52 minutes past the hour, from 7:07 a.m. to 8:07 p.m. I caught the 10:07, and it is a very good start to a day of yokai.

## Rent the ¥500 e-bike

The rental is right by the station, and it cost me ¥500. My deadline to bring it back was 6 p.m., which was plenty. Check the <a href="https://fukusakikankou.jp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fukusaki tourism association's site</a> for current rental hours and closing days before you go.

Get the electric one. The hand-drawn map is adorable, but it is not to scale, and a couple of the benches are a real ride from the station. One of them is up a hill. On a regular bike I would have been a puddle by yokai number nine.

## The trick: every bench is on Google Maps

Here is where I went wrong. I followed the paper map in order, rode right past two benches without noticing, and only figured it out when I got to the next stop. Then I had to backtrack.

What fixed it: every bench is pinned on Google Maps. Search "Yokai Bench:" and the yokai's name, tap the pin, and get bike directions. Once I started doing that, the rest of the trail went fast.

Two things to know. Google spells a few of the names differently than the map does, so if a search comes up empty, try Konakijijii, Sentakukitsune, or Sunakakebabaa. And the Kappa-Komahiki pin is listed as Kappakomabiki, with no "Yokai Bench:" in front of it.

## My favorites

Every bench has its own little bit, and half the fun is seeing what each yokai is up to. A few that made me stop and laugh:

**Tengu** is a mountain spirit with a long nose, wings, and magic powers. Anything strange that happened in the mountains got blamed on him. In Fukusaki he is wearing a blue suit and typing on a laptop, really interested in whatever is happening on the internet. I can relate.

**Oni** is the one on the hill, and the one in the photo at the top. Folklore has one famous oni standing 6 meters tall with five horns and fifteen eyes, eventually taken down with poisoned sake. This one was just taking a selfie when I walked up. How's it going, bro?

**Azukiarai**, the red bean washer, sits by the river washing beans and singing about whether to keep washing or catch someone to eat. Fukusaki's version is a green guy in a tank top and a gold chain, deep into a game on his Switch.

## Before you go

- **Bring a phone mount.** I didn't have one, and you will be checking directions at every turn. The one I wish I'd had is in the gear list below.
- **Charge your phone.** You will have Google Maps open for three hours and you will take a ridiculous number of bench selfies.
- **Scan the QR codes.** Each one links to that yokai's story. It is the best part of the trail, and the bench poses make a lot more sense once you know who you are sitting next to.
- **Go in daylight.** The trail is spread across town, and you want light to find the benches and to read the map you are not going to use.
- **Watch the kickstand.** My bike tipped over at the Umibozu bench. Park on flat ground before you go in for the selfie.

## Make it a day with Himeji Castle

You change trains at Himeji either way, and Himeji Castle is one of the best castles in Japan. It is an easy walk from the station. If you want to turn the trip into a full day, or stay the night in Himeji, a guided tour gets you the history without the guesswork.
