Seoul Forest (서울숲) gets called Seoul's answer to Central Park. I live in New York, so I had to check. Five days into Seoul, I needed something green, so I spent a Monday morning walking it: past a waterfall, up to the sika deer, over a footbridge through the treetops and straight across an expressway, down to the Han River, and back through the wetlands. Central Park has squirrels. Seoul Forest has deer.

Here is how to get there, the route I walked, what to know before you go, and how it scored against Central Park.

## Seoul Forest at a glance

- **Take the subway to Seoul Forest Station.** It is on the Suin-Bundang Line, and Exit 3 puts you about a five-minute walk from the park. Ttukseom Station on Line 2, Exit 8, is the other option, about ten minutes on foot.
- **It is free and open around the clock.** Some parts keep their own hours, so see the tips below.
- **Follow the deer signs.** The sika deer live in a fenced yard in the eco forest, and you can watch them from the path and from the footbridge above.
- **Walk the footbridge to the Han River.** It runs up into the trees and then over the expressway, and the park continues to the river on the other side.
- **Give it an hour or two.** My loop from the forest to the river and back took about an hour and a quarter, at a slow pace with a lot of stopping.

## How to get to Seoul Forest

The easiest way is the Suin-Bundang Line to **Seoul Forest Station** (서울숲역). Take **Exit 3**, and the park entrance is about a five-minute walk away.

If you are on Line 2, get off at **Ttukseom Station** (뚝섬역) and take **Exit 8**. It is about a ten-minute walk, and it puts you closer to the Seongsu side of the neighborhood if you want coffee first.

The park is free, and it does not close. The land has had a few lives before this: a royal hunting ground, a water treatment plant, a racetrack, and a golf course. In 2005, it became a park.

## The route I walked

### 1. The Waterfall of Wishes

The forest side of the park starts out quiet: stone ponds, wooded paths, a rocky stream. One of the first things I found was the Waterfall of Wishes (소원의 폭포). I did not make a wish. In hindsight, I should have wished for less wind on the bridges.

### 2. Follow the signs to the sika deer

You don't need a map for this part. Signs with a little deer on them point the way, and the last one says the deer are 50 meters to the right.

The info sign by the yard has the facts. Sika deer stand about 92 centimeters at the shoulder. The bucks grow antlers in April and drop them in November, and breeding season runs from October to December. I was there in mid-October, so: good timing?

The deer live in a big fenced yard with a very clear "please don't feed the animals" sign. This is not Nara. Nobody is getting headbutted for a cracker. While I was there, one walked right up to the fence and lowered its head, which is the closest I have ever been to a deer in a city.

### 3. Up the footbridge into the trees

Right by the deer yard, a footbridge climbs up into the canopy. You can look down into the deer yard from up there, and then the bridge keeps going through the treetops, over willows and a wetland. It was very windy up there when I went.

### 5. Down to the Han River

The park runs all the way to the Han River. A ramp takes you down through tall trees to the riverside path, with bike lanes, cyclists, and bridges in every direction. There is a red observation deck that sticks out over the water. The rules sign there is only in Korean, so my phone had to read it for me. Bring a translation app with camera mode.

On the way back along the water, I was hoping to find some fall colors. I found one (1) turtle. His name is Mr. Turtle Friend, because I named him. Central Park has a whole pond named after its turtles. Seoul Forest has Mr. Turtle Friend. He's doing his best.

## Seoul Forest vs. Central Park: the final score

I scored it the only fair way: six completely scientific rounds.

- **Size: Central Park.** Seoul Forest is about 1.2 square kilometers. Central Park is about 3.4, so roughly three times bigger.
- **Wildlife: Seoul Forest.** Deer beat squirrels.
- **Bridges: tie.** Seoul Forest has a footbridge through the treetops and over an expressway. Bow Bridge is prettier. Sorry.
- **Water views: Seoul Forest.** The park runs right down to the Han River.
- **Turtles: Central Park.** Central Park named a whole pond after its turtles. Seoul Forest has one (1) turtle.
- **Trees that stare back: Seoul Forest.** Googly eyes. Uncontested.

**Final score: Seoul Forest 3, Central Park 2, one tie.** I stand by every call except possibly Bridges. Which round would you have scored differently?

## Before you go

- **Some parts keep their own hours.** The park itself never closes, but the eco forest, where the deer and the footbridge are, keeps shorter hours. Most listings say roughly 5:30 a.m. to 9:30 p.m., though they don't all agree, so check before you go late. The Insect Botanical Garden and the butterfly garden close on Mondays.
- **A weekday morning is a good time.** I went around 10 a.m. on a Monday, and it was quiet: a couple watching the deer, a few cyclists down by the river, and me.
- **Don't feed the deer.** The signs are very clear about it.
- **Bring a jacket for the bridges.** It was calm in the trees and very windy on the footbridge and over the expressway.
- **Go in mid to late October for the start of fall color.** I went on October 19, and the reds and oranges were just coming in. Later in the fall should have more.
- **Have a translation app ready.** The signs around the deer have English, but not every sign does, like the one on the observation deck.

## Make it a day in Seongsu

Seoul Forest sits right next to Seongsu-dong, the neighborhood with the towers you see over the wetland. Seongsu is known for its cafes, shops, and pop-up stores, so it is an easy place to keep going after the park. If you came in through Ttukseom Station, you are already halfway there.
